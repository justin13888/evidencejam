# Renderer review: 20 answered texts against their cited edges (ROADMAP item 15)

Done criterion: "20 sampled answers are reviewed against their cited edges with zero overclaims."
Each item shows the answer text (renderer `faithful`) next to every cited edge's `original_sentence`,
grouped by study, with the edge's subject, predicate and object as stored in the graph. Regenerate with
`python experiments/renderer/review_sample.py` (seeded, stratified by direction and by whether every
counted vote is associational). The reviewer verdicts are recorded below the items.

Rules the reviewer applied (and only these):

- **D1.** Edges with predicate `associated_with` or `correlated_with` are associational. When *every*
  cited edge of an answer is associational, the text must say "is associated with" (or "association")
  and never use causal wording (causes, increases, decreases, leads to, reduces, raises, improves,
  effect). When at least one cited edge is causal, causal wording is allowed.
- **Overclaim** = the text claims more than the sentences support: causal wording for associational
  evidence, a direction the sentences don't show, wrong study counts, hedges stronger or weaker than the
  stated certainty. The stated certainty is the only hedge: high = no modifier, moderate = "probably",
  low = "may", very low = "The evidence is very uncertain about ...". "No detectable effect" means the
  study tested and found no significant difference (a null), not a decrease. Studies are counted once each
  (several edges from one study are one study). The direction is the effect on the question's outcome as
  the question's cause goes up or is present; a node marked [decreased] or [removed] flips the sign.
